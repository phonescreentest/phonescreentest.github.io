What is an iPhone screen test?

An iPhone screen test is a free online check that inspects your iPhone display for dead pixels, stuck pixels, lines, uneven colour and touch problems. Modern iPhones use Super Retina OLED displays, while Pro models add ProMotion with a 120 Hz refresh rate for smoother scrolling, and True Tone adjusts the white balance to match the light around you. Ceramic Shield glass protects the front of newer iPhones, but the display underneath can still be damaged by a hard drop or poor-quality repair. This test shows full-screen solid colours and a touch grid, so you can look over the whole panel, including the area around the notch or Dynamic Island.

The test is especially useful when buying a used iPhone, because a screen fault is one of the most expensive things to fix. A two-minute check before you pay can reveal a green or white vertical line, burn-in, or a dead zone the seller never mentioned.

How to run a screen test on your iPhone

  1. Turn True Tone off for the test. In Settings, under Display and Brightness, switch True Tone off so colour shifts do not hide a fault.
  2. Set brightness to maximum. Drag the brightness slider to full and turn off auto-brightness so the screen stays bright while you inspect it.
  3. Open the test in Safari. Tap start and let the test fill the whole screen, hiding the browser bar.
  4. Inspect each solid colour. Study the white, black, red, green, blue and grey screens for dots, lines, dark patches or colour tint.
  5. Run the touch grid. Tap every cell, including the corners and edges near the home indicator, and watch for cells that stay unmarked.
  6. Drag across the screen. Draw a slow line from edge to edge and check that it follows your finger smoothly with no breaks or jumps.

Common iPhone display problems this test can reveal

  • Dead or stuck pixels that show as tiny black, white or coloured dots on a solid background.
  • Green or white vertical lines, a known fault on some iPhone OLED displays, often appearing after a drop or a repair.
  • Touch dead zones where taps, swipes or typing are ignored, commonly near the edges or in one corner.
  • Uneven brightness or a yellow or green tint that is stronger on one side of the screen.
  • Burn-in or image retention, where faint outlines of the keyboard or status bar linger on plain colours.

Tips for an accurate iPhone screen test

  • Clean the screen with a soft cloth first, so fingerprints and dust are not mistaken for dead pixels.
  • Test in a dim room, where faint lines and subtle tint are much easier to notice.
  • Remove the case and screen protector if you suspect a touch fault, then test again to see if the problem changes.
  • Check at both low and high brightness, since some faults, such as tint or flicker, only appear at one end of the range.

Frequently asked questions

Does this iPhone screen test work on all iPhone models?

Yes. It runs in Safari on any iPhone, from older models with LCD screens to the latest Pro models with Super Retina OLED and ProMotion.

Can I use this test to check a used iPhone before buying it?

Yes, it is one of the best quick checks you can do. Run the colour and touch tests on the phone itself and look for lines, dead pixels and dead zones before you pay.

What should I do if I see a green line on my iPhone screen?

A green vertical line usually means a fault in the OLED panel or its connector. Restart the phone first, but if the line stays, the screen normally needs replacing, so check warranty or AppleCare cover.

Can this test find a problem with a replaced iPhone screen?

Yes. Aftermarket or poorly fitted screens often show uneven colour, weaker brightness, touch dead zones or ghost touch, and this test makes those problems easy to spot.

Is the iPhone screen test really free?

Yes. It runs in your browser with no payment, no sign-up and no app to install, and works on iPhone, iPad and any other device with a web browser.
